## Question 1: Mission & Vision
*What is your mission or artistic vision and what do you do to support it?*

We see all people — especially the ones others ignore. That belief is the foundation of everything we do.

50+TechBridge exists to close the gap between Austin's older adults and the AI-driven world they live in — not by pushing technology on people, but by building community and creative identity around it.

Our vision is a city where age is not a barrier to creative and cultural participation. Artificial intelligence is the defining creative medium of this era — it is reshaping how art is made, how stories are told, how people connect, and how communities protect themselves. If older Austinites cannot access or understand these tools, they are effectively excluded from the cultural life of the city they built.

We support this mission through eight-week Digital Pioneer cohorts, each moving participants through three stages of creative development:

**Stage 1 — Meet & Begin (Week 1):** A free, 2-hour in-person public gathering at an Austin library or senior center. Open to all. No device required. Participants meet their cohort, take their first step with AI tools, and are assigned a one-on-one volunteer mentor who will walk with them through the full eight weeks.

**Stage 2 — Learn & Create (Weeks 2–6):** Hands-on AI training paired with weekly online mastermind sessions. Participants move from learning the tools to using them with intention — building the creative work they will one day display. Mentor support continues throughout. This is where the real work happens: peer-to-peer, in their own voices, at their own pace.

**Stage 3 — Exhibit & Celebrate (Weeks 7–8):** Participants refine their work, then display it publicly. Week 8 is You Did It — each completed design is exhibited at the Digital Pioneer Gallery, hosted at one of Austin's cultural institutions. The Gallery is open to the public. A silent auction gives the community the opportunity to purchase the work, with all proceeds going directly to the artist.

Our artistic values are accessibility, authenticity, and dignity. We meet every person at their own level. We honor the wisdom they already carry. Because belonging in modern Austin — and being seen as an artist — should not have a price tag.

50+TechBridge is the only program in Austin using AI as a creative medium to serve adults 50+. The Digital Pioneer Gallery follows a model Austin already believes in: participants create work, display it publicly, and keep what it earns. We have simply brought that model into the digital age — and into the hands of the generation that built this city.

## Question 2: Proposed Activity
*What is your proposed activity and how does it reflect the people, places, or cultures of Austin?*

We are proposing eight Digital Pioneer cohorts — free, 8-week creative community journeys delivered across underserved Austin neighborhoods, culminating in a public exhibition at one of Austin's cultural institutions.

**The Cohort Arc:**

**Week 1 — In-Person Launch:** A free public gathering at an Austin Public Library branch or senior center in neighborhoods including East Austin, Rundberg, North Loop, Southeast Austin, and South Congress. Participants are introduced to AI as a creative tool through their own stories and goals. No experience required. Walk-ins welcome. Every participant meets their one-on-one volunteer mentor.

**Weeks 2–3 — Learn Your Tools:** Hands-on training with the AI tools participants will use throughout the program. Mentor relationships deepen. Small group sessions build confidence and creative momentum.

**Weeks 4–6 — Create:** Participants build something real — a digital artwork, a family history, a story, a design. Mentor support is available every step. The weekly mastermind keeps the cohort together and moving.

**Week 7 — Refine:** Participants finalize their work and prepare for the public exhibition.

**Week 8 — You Did It:** Completed works are displayed at the Digital Pioneer Gallery — a public exhibition hosted at one of Austin's cultural institutions, open to the entire community. A silent auction gives attendees the opportunity to purchase the work. All proceeds go directly to the artist.

**Why This Reflects Austin:**

This model is built around the people Austin often leaves behind. East Austin's historically Black and Latino communities. North Austin's aging immigrant families. Southeast Austin's working-class seniors who helped build this city. We go to their libraries, their senior centers, their neighborhoods. We don't ask them to come to us.

The Gallery brings them to the cultural institutions that have not always come to them — and puts their work on the wall.

The mastermind model honors something Austin values: community wisdom. Participants are not students being filled with information. They are Digital Pioneers — people whose lived experience of Austin is itself the most valuable asset in the room.

The Digital Pioneer Gallery extends this cultural investment into the city's public life. When an older Austinite's AI-assisted artwork hangs in a cultural venue — and sells — that is a creative and cultural outcome Austin has not seen before.

## Question 3: Experience
*Describe your experience producing creative work or offering public experiences in Austin.*

Since founding 50+TechBridge in Austin, Brian McKinney has designed and facilitated more than 30 free public experiences across Austin's library and senior center network. These are open-door events — no registration barriers, no prerequisites, no technology required.

To date we have reached 264+ adults 50+ across 13 community locations, including:
- Austin Public Library branches: St. John, Rundberg, Southeast, Milwood, Manchaca Road, Pleasant Hill, North Village, Windsor Park, and Old Quarry
- Senior centers: Gus Garcia, Onion Creek, and Foundation Communities

Each experience is designed and facilitated by Brian McKinney, who brings a background in technology education, community organizing, and adult learning. He developed 50+TechBridge's facilitation model specifically for older adults who are new to or skeptical of technology — grounded in patience, humor, and real-world application.

Our sessions have produced measurable cultural outcomes. Participants have used AI tools to write memoirs and family histories, communicate with grandchildren, navigate Medicare, and protect themselves from AI-driven fraud. Many participants have returned to multiple sessions, bringing neighbors and friends.

We are also developing a partnership with UT Austin's geriatric programs to bring students studying senior adult care, gerontology, and health sciences into our cohorts as volunteer mentors. This intergenerational model gives older participants dedicated one-on-one support while giving students hands-on field experience — creating mutual benefit that strengthens both the program and the next generation of professionals who will serve the 50+ community.

We have built trusted relationships with branch librarians, senior center coordinators, and cultural institution partners across Austin. These relationships — built through consistent presence and genuine community trust — are how we fill every session and how we know the cohort model and the Digital Pioneer Gallery will succeed.

Our program architecture — library-based delivery, volunteer one-on-one mentorship, and a structured cohort model that ends in a public exhibition where artists keep what their work earns — combines elements Austin has funded and proven at scale across multiple programs. We are applying that same proven structure to the one population no existing program reaches: adults 50+, learning AI as a creative tool.

LearnMoreTechnologies.com also operates SecureStart.app, a live digital safety platform built for adults 50+, demonstrating our technical capacity to deliver both in-person and digital program components at scale.

## Question 5: Marketing Plan
*How will you market your proposed activities to Austin residents, visitors, and tourists, including underserved communities?*

Our marketing strategy is community-first and channel-specific. We go where our audience already is — because our audience is not on Instagram, and they do not respond to digital ads.

**Library and Senior Center Networks (primary)**
We work directly with Austin Public Library branch managers and senior center program coordinators to post flyers, add cohorts to their event calendars, and make personal announcements at existing programming. These partners have direct relationships with our exact audience — their endorsement is the most effective outreach we have.

**Agentic50 Weekly Newsletter**
Our founder publishes a weekly AI literacy newsletter for adults 50+ with an active Austin subscriber base. Every cohort is announced four weeks in advance with plain-language descriptions and a clear walk-in welcome message.

**Spanish-Language and Community Radio**
We partner with KAZI 88.7 FM and Spanish-language radio and print outlets to announce cohorts in East and Southeast Austin — neighborhoods where many older residents are first-generation immigrants who don't use digital channels.

**Faith Communities and Neighborhood Organizations**
We have existing relationships with Foundation Communities, Gus Garcia Senior Center, and neighborhood associations across Austin's older adult network. These trusted community voices pre-announce each cohort to people who have no relationship with libraries or technology but trust their community organizations.

**Digital Pioneer Gallery — Public Exhibition Promotion**
The Gallery is promoted through Austin cultural institution partner channels, CreateAustin.org, and the Austin Public Library public event calendar. Gallery events are designed to draw general Austin audiences — not just the 50+ community — creating cross-generational public attendance and press opportunities.

**Walk-In Welcome Policy**
Every in-person launch is explicitly walk-in friendly — no registration, no device required. Our marketing always leads with: "You don't need anything to come. Just show up."

**For Visitors and Tourists**
Cohort launches and Gallery exhibitions are listed on CreateAustin.org, distributed to hotels and visitor centers citywide. Any Austin visitor is welcome to attend.
