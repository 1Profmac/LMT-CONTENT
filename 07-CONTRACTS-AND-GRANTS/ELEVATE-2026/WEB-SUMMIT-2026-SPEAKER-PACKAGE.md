## SESSION DESCRIPTION (300 words — paste directly into application)

Every AI displacement story ends the same way: a statistic.

47% of jobs at risk. 85 million jobs eliminated by 2025. Trillions in economic disruption.

What the statistics leave out is the face. The 58-year-old marketing director told her skills
are obsolete. The 62-year-old logistics manager restructured out after 31 years. The 54-year-old
teacher who built a career in a classroom and is now watching a chatbot do it cheaper.

Adults 50 and older are being displaced by AI at a rate that no workforce policy is addressing,
no retraining program is reaching, and no stage is talking about — at least not from their
perspective.

Brian McKinney is the founder of Digital Pioneer, an AI education and business-building program
for adults 50 and older. He teaches in Austin Public Library branches, faith communities, and
senior centers. His students are not statistics. They are entrepreneurs, caregivers, grandparents,
and professionals who spent three decades building expertise that companies are now calling a
liability — and who are using AI to build something no company can take from them: an independent
income, a digital presence, and a business of their own.

This session is not about the problem. It is about what happens when you stop waiting for
corporations or governments to solve it — and put the tools directly in the hands of the people
the economy left behind.

Attendees will leave with:
- The documented cost of AI displacement on the 50+ workforce — numbers policy makers are not using
- A replicable model for AI education that reaches the communities most at risk
- A framework for building economic independence using the same AI tools that caused the displacement
- The argument for why experience + AI is not redundant — it is the most powerful combination in the workforce

This is not a future problem. This is 2026. And the window is now.

## SPEAKER BIO — LONG (400 words — for press, podcast bookings, event programs)

Brian McKinney spent decades in corporate America watching companies make decisions that treated
experienced workers as costs to be managed rather than assets to be invested in. When AI accelerated
that trend into a crisis, he did not write a white paper. He built a program.

McKinney is the founder of 50+TechBridge and Digital Pioneer — an AI education and business-building
initiative for adults 50 and older operating in Austin, Texas, with a growing international digital
community. He teaches in Austin Public Library branches, faith communities, and senior centers —
directly in the rooms where displaced workers are trying to figure out what comes next.

His program does not offer sympathy. It offers tools. Students learn to use AI to write business
plans, build online storefronts, create content, develop marketing strategies, and launch businesses
that generate real income. Many of his students have never used AI for any purpose. Most have been
told — explicitly or implicitly — that the digital economy is not for them. McKinney's work is a
direct argument against that lie.

His research and advocacy focus on three interconnected forces reshaping the 50+ workforce:

1. Companies using AI to systematically displace experienced workers while calling it efficiency
2. The complete absence of retraining infrastructure for the demographic most affected
3. A digital world designed by 28-year-olds for 28-year-olds — inaccessible by design to the
   people who need it most

McKinney is the creator of the "What If" brand film and content series — a cinematic manifesto
that imagines a world where companies, communities, and systems made a different choice. Where
experience was treated as intellectual property. Where 50+ adults were the AI advantage, not
the liability.

He is building Digital Pioneer into a global community with a 12-month target of 50,000 members
across six platforms, English and Spanish-language programming, and a peer ambassador model
designed to reach communities traditional workforce programs never touch — including Black and
Hispanic 50+ adults, the demographic hardest hit by AI-driven displacement and the least served
by existing solutions.

McKinney has been called "the voice the AI conversation is missing" — and he is positioning that
voice for world stages in 2026 because, as he says: "Three years is too long. AI displacement is
happening at exponential speed. The window is now."

Speaking inquiries: digitalpioneer.ai
Podcast bookings: agentic50.org
