## The Framing (arts/culture language for tech workshops)

### What we call it:
"Technology Assisted Trainings for Adults 50+" — publicly accessible creative technology programming

### Workshop Model:
- Modeled after AARP's community workshop format — conducted at libraries and community centers across greater Austin
- Same trusted, accessible, public venue model that AARP uses — but focused on AI and creative digital skills
- LMT fills the gap AARP doesn't cover: hands-on AI literacy and creative technology for 50+ adults

### How to describe it for AACME:
- Technology Assisted Trainings that empower Austin's 50+ community to participate in the creative digital economy
- Publicly accessible programming at libraries and senior centers across greater Austin — same model as AARP community workshops
- Bridging the cultural digital divide — ensuring older adults aren't excluded from the creative possibilities of AI and technology
- Creative technology education: digital storytelling, AI-powered content creation, online self-expression, and digital communication arts

### Key language to use (mirrors Nexus-winning approach):
- "Technology Assisted Trainings" — positions LMT as next-level, structured, AI-powered education (not casual "workshops")
- "Publicly accessible" — all trainings are free and open to Austin residents
- "Creative production" — teaching adults to create digital content, tell their stories, express themselves through technology
- "Cultural programming" — digital literacy as a cultural practice, not just job training
- "Community engagement" — partnerships with 9 libraries, 3 senior centers across 12 Austin locations
- "Arts service provider" — LMT supports creatives 50+ in developing digital creative skills

### What NOT to say:
- Don't lead with "tech training" or "workforce development" — that's WIOA language, not AACME
- Don't say "business skills" or "professional development"
- Don't frame it as job prep — frame it as creative empowerment and cultural participation

## Proof Points (verified, same as Nexus)
- 9 libraries, 3 senior centers, 200 adults, 12 locations
- Nexus grant recipient 2024 — established relationship with AACME
- 2+ years of creative production history in Austin
- All programming is free, public, and advertised to Austin residents

- Elevate requires 2 years production history (Brian has it)
- Elevate has more reporting requirements
- Same portal (Submittable), same admin (Create Austin at Long Center)
