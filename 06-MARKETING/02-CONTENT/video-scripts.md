## MODULE 1, LESSON 2: Know Which Mode to Use
**Duration: 3 minutes**

Claude has four modes and most people only know about one of them. That's like owning a Swiss Army knife and only using the toothpick.

Let me break them down.

Chat. That's the one you probably know. You type a question, Claude answers. It's fine for quick things. What's the capital of Peru. How do I convert a PDF. Nothing serious.

Projects. This is for teams. If you have recurring deliverables — a weekly report, a monthly newsletter — and multiple people need access, Projects is your space.

Code. This is for software developers. If you write code for a living, this is powerful. If you don't, skip it entirely.

And then there's Cowork. This is the one that changes everything.

Cowork is for deep solo work with actual files on your computer. Claude reads your documents. It follows your rules. It writes outputs to a folder you choose. It's not a conversation — it's a collaboration.

If you've only been using Chat, you've been using the ChatGPT version of Claude. That's over. From today forward, you live in Cowork.

Open the app right now. Find Cowork mode. Click into it. Spend fifteen minutes just exploring what's there. That's your assignment for today.

## MODULE 2, LESSON 1: Build Your 4 Folders
**Duration: 3 minutes**

Before you ask Claude to do anything, you need to give it a workspace. You wouldn't hire someone and say sit anywhere, figure it out. You'd give them a desk, a filing system, and some rules.

That's what we're doing right now.

Go to your computer. Create one folder. Call it Claude-Cowork. That's your parent folder. Everything lives inside it.

Now create four subfolders.

First, ABOUT ME. This is where you tell Claude who you are. Your identity, your writing style, your rules. We'll fill this in tomorrow.

Second, PROJECTS. One subfolder for every live project you're working on. Each project gets a brief, a drafts folder, and a references folder.

Third, TEMPLATES. This is where you save your best past work — not the content, just the structure. If you wrote a great proposal last year, save the bones of it here. Claude can use that structure for new work.

Fourth, CLAUDE OUTPUTS. This is the only folder Claude is allowed to write to. Everything else is read-only. You don't want Claude overwriting your source files. It reads from three folders. It writes to one. Clean, controlled, professional.

That's it. Four folders. One rule. Download Worksheet 1 and check off each folder as you create it. Take a screenshot when you're done. That's today's assignment.

## MODULE 3, LESSON 1: Write Your About Me File
**Duration: 4 minutes**

This is the lesson that saves you fifty prompts.

Right now, every time you use Claude, you probably start from scratch. You explain who you are, what you need, how you want it written. Every single time.

That ends today.

You're going to write one file called about-me.md and put it in your ABOUT ME folder. From now on, Claude reads this file before it does anything. It already knows who you are. It already knows your style. It already knows what matters.

So what goes in it?

Start with who you are. Your name, your title, your company. But don't write a resume. Write it like you're briefing a new coworker on their first day. Here's who I am. Here's what I do. Here's what matters right now.

Then write what you're working on. Your top three priorities. Not your whole life story — just what's on your plate this week or this month.

Then write how you communicate. Are you formal or casual? Direct or warm? What words do you use a lot? Who's your audience?

Then write what matters to you. Your values. Your expertise. The things that should come through in everything Claude writes for you.

And finally, write your rules. What should Claude always do? What should it never do? Be specific. If you hate bullet points, say so. If you always want short paragraphs, say so.

Download Worksheet 2. Fill in every section. Then type it up as a markdown file and save it to your ABOUT ME folder. That's half of today's work done.

## MODULE 3, LESSON 2: Write Your Anti-AI Style Guide
**Duration: 3 minutes**

You know that feeling when you read something and you just know AI wrote it? That robotic, overly polished, says-nothing-while-sounding-smart feeling?

That's what this file kills.

Create a file called anti-ai-style.md. Put it in your ABOUT ME folder right next to the one you just made. This file is a list of every word and phrase Claude is never allowed to use in your content. Ever.

Let me give you some starters.

Boundaries. Delve. Navigate. Leverage. Utilize. It's worth noting. In today's world. At the end of the day. Game-changer. Deep dive. Unpack. Robust. Landscape. Streamline. Cutting-edge. Paradigm shift.

You know these phrases. They make everything sound like a corporate memo written by a robot. They're gone.

But don't stop there. Add your own. What phrases make you cringe? What words does your industry overuse? What would your best friend call you out for if they read it?

And here's the important part — don't just ban phrases. Tell Claude what to say instead. Instead of leverage, say use. Instead of navigate, say figure out. Instead of it's worth noting, just say the thing.

Download Worksheet 3. Circle the phrases you want banned. Add your own. Write your replacements. Then type it up and save it.

Between this file and your about-me.md, Claude now knows who you are and how you sound. That's more powerful than any prompt you'll ever write.

## MODULE 4, LESSON 1: Build Your Prompt Template
**Duration: 3 minutes**

Here's where most people waste time. They sit down, stare at Claude, and try to write the perfect prompt. Every time. From scratch.

Stop doing that.

Instead, build one prompt template that you reuse for everything. It's a starting point that handles the basics so you can focus on the task.

Here's what your template should say. Read my about-me.md and anti-ai-style.md before starting. Write all outputs to the CLAUDE OUTPUTS folder. Name files using this convention — project name, client name, version number, file type. Then, ask me what I need done before you start.

That's it. Four instructions. You use this every time.

If you're on a Mac, you can save this as a text shortcut. Type slash prompt and it expands into the full template. On Windows, you can save it as a text file and paste it in.

The point is you never start from zero again. You open Cowork, paste your template, and tell Claude what you need. The setup is handled. The style is handled. The file organization is handled. You just do the work.

Try it right now. Paste your template into Cowork. Give Claude a simple task — rewrite an email, summarize a document, draft a LinkedIn post. See what comes out. It should already sound like you.

## MODULE 4, LESSON 2: Let Claude Prompt You
**Duration: 4 minutes**

This is the lesson that changes how you think about AI.

Up until now, you've been the one doing all the talking. You write the prompt. You explain what you want. You try to get the words right.

Now we flip it.

Instead of you prompting Claude, let Claude prompt you. Tell Claude what kind of output you need — a report, a proposal, an email, a presentation — and then say, ask me the questions you need answered before you start.

Watch what happens. Claude will ask you smart, specific questions. Who's the audience? What's the goal? What tone? How long? What must be included? What should be avoided?

You just answer. Click, click, click. It takes less than a minute.

Then Claude builds a plan. It shows you what it's going to do. You review it. You approve it. It executes.

This is faster than writing a prompt. It's more accurate because Claude asked the right questions. And it eliminates the blank-page problem that stops most people from using AI consistently.

You don't need to be a prompt engineer. You need to be a decision-maker. And that's a skill you've been building your entire career. You're already good at this. You just didn't know it applied here.

Try it now. Open Cowork, paste your template, and say — I need a one-page summary of what I accomplished this week. Ask me questions before you start. Then just answer what Claude asks. See how it feels.

## MODULE 5, LESSON 1: Install Your First Plugin
**Duration: 3 minutes**

You've got Claude set up. It knows who you are. It knows how you write. Now let's make it a specialist.

Plugins are add-ons that give Claude specific skills for specific jobs. Think of it like hiring a generalist and then giving them a certification. They can still do everything — but now they're really good at one thing.

Here's the rule. Install one. Just one. Don't browse the whole plugin store. Don't install five things and try to learn them all at once. One.

Pick the plugin that matches what you do most. If you spend most of your day writing — emails, reports, content — install the Marketing plugin. If you live in spreadsheets and dashboards, install the Data plugin. If you review contracts or legal documents, install the Legal plugin.

Once it's installed, you get shortcuts. Things like slash marketing colon draft-content. Or slash data colon explore. These are specific commands that tell Claude exactly what kind of work you're doing.

Try your first shortcut. Give Claude a real task from your actual job. Not a test — real work. See what it produces.

If you filled out Worksheet 4, you already know which plugin to pick. If you didn't, go back and do it now. It takes five minutes and it'll save you from overthinking this.

One plugin. Learn it well. You can always add another later.

## MODULE 5, LESSON 2: Connect Your First Tool
**Duration: 3 minutes**

This is the difference between Claude working in a box and Claude working in your business.

Up until now, Claude has been reading files from your computer. That's powerful. But you probably also work in Gmail, Google Docs, Slack, Notion, or a dozen other tools. Wouldn't it be nice if Claude could reach into those tools while it's working?

That's what Connectors do.

Go to Settings. Click Connectors. Click Browse. Click Add. Find the tool you use most and connect it.

Now here's the important distinction. Plugins are tools you use inside Claude. You run a command and Claude does something. Connectors are the opposite. Connectors let Claude reach into the tools you already use. It can search your Slack messages. It can pull information from your Google Docs. It can reference your Notion pages in the middle of a task.

Plugins give Claude new abilities. Connectors give Claude access to your world.

Pick one tool. The one you use every day. Connect it. Then give Claude a task that requires information from that tool. Say something like — look at my last five emails from this client and draft a follow-up. Or — pull the notes from my last team meeting in Notion and create action items.

That's when Claude stops feeling like a separate app and starts feeling like a teammate who has access to everything you do.

## MODULE 6, LESSON 1: Build Your First Project
**Duration: 4 minutes**

Everything you've built so far — the folders, the files, the template, the plugin, the connector — it all comes together in a Project.

A Project is a workspace for a specific, recurring deliverable. Maybe it's your weekly newsletter. Maybe it's a monthly client report. Maybe it's a proposal template you customize for each prospect. Whatever you produce regularly — that's your first project.

Here's how to set it up.

Go to your PROJECTS folder inside Claude-Cowork. Create a new subfolder with the project name. Inside that subfolder, create three things.

A file called brief.md. This tells Claude what the project is about. What's the goal? What's the deliverable? What tone should it use? What must be included? What should be avoided? What does success look like? If you filled out Worksheet 5, you've already written this.

A folder called references. Put examples in here. Past versions. Source material. Anything Claude should look at for context.

A folder called drafts. This is where work in progress lives before the final version goes to CLAUDE OUTPUTS.

Now run your first deliverable. Open Cowork, paste your template, and say — I'm working on the project in this folder. Read the brief. Read the references. Produce the deliverable.

Watch what happens. Claude reads your brief. It reads your style guide. It looks at your examples. It produces something that actually sounds like you, formatted the way you want, following your rules.

That's not a chatbot response. That's a work product. And you can run it again next week, next month, as many times as you need.

## MODULE 7, LESSON 1: Schedule Your First Automated Task
**Duration: 3 minutes**

This is the endgame.

Everything you've done this week has been you sitting at your computer, telling Claude what to do. That's already powerful. But what if Claude could do the work while you sleep?

That's what scheduling does.

Use the schedule plugin to set up a task that runs on a timer. It could be daily. It could be weekly. You decide.

Here are some ideas for your first automated task. A morning briefing that summarizes your email and calendar before you wake up. A weekly content draft that pulls from your project folder and has a first version ready by Monday morning. A daily data summary that checks your spreadsheets and highlights what changed.

Set up one task. Just one. Pick something you do every week that takes you thirty minutes or more. Automate it.

Then check the output. Did Claude follow your style guide? Did it write to the right folder? Did it produce something you can actually use — or at least something that gets you eighty percent of the way there?

That eighty percent is the point. You're not replacing yourself. You're giving yourself a head start. Every morning you wake up to a first draft that's already in your voice, already formatted, already following your rules. You spend ten minutes editing instead of an hour creating.

That's not a tool. That's a team member who works the night shift.
