## THE HEADLINE

### The Real Reason Tech Training Fails Adults 50+

Here's something I've heard from every single organization I've worked with:

"Our older participants just aren't motivated to learn technology."

I've heard it from workforce boards. Libraries. Healthcare systems. Employers. They all say the same thing — and they're all wrong.

After delivering 50+TechBridge at libraries, senior centers, and a veterans workshop across Austin, I can tell you exactly what the problem is. It's not motivation. It's design. Most tech training was built for younger learners and handed to older ones without changing a thing. Same slides, same pace, same 24-year-old instructor who's never struggled with technology a day in their life.

When we changed the model — peer instructors, project-based learning, no jargon — completion rates hit targeted 3X the industry average. Same zip codes. Same demographics. Different approach.

**The bottom line:** Adults 50+ don't have a motivation problem. They have a training problem. And the data proves it.

## THE CHECKLIST

### This Week's Audience: Workforce Professionals

**"Is Your 50+ Training Actually Working? A 5-Minute Audit"**

- [ ] Pull your completion rate for participants aged 50+. Is it above 60%? (Industry average is under 20%.)
- [ ] Ask your last 10 participants over 50: "Did you use what you learned the week after training ended?" If fewer than half say yes, your training isn't sticking.
- [ ] Check who's teaching. Is your instructor closer in age to the participants, or to their grandchildren? Peer instruction changes everything.
- [ ] Look at your curriculum. Are participants building something real — or just clicking through slides and taking a quiz?
- [ ] Count how many participants came back for a second program. Retention tells you more than satisfaction surveys.

**If you scored 3 or fewer:** Your 50+ population deserves better. That's not a criticism — it's an opportunity. Reply to this email and I'll share what we've learned.
