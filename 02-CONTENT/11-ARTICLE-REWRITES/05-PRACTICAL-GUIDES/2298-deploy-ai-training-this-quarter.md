<h2>Why Cohorts — Not Courses — Are the Answer</h2>
<p>Before we get to the playbook, you need to understand why this works when other programs don&#8217;t.</p>
<p>Self-paced online courses have a <strong>3-5% completion rate</strong>. That&#8217;s not a typo. For every 100 people who start, 95 quit. For adults 50+ who already feel uncertain about technology, self-paced courses are a guaranteed failure.</p>
<p>Cohort-based learning — where a group moves through the program together with shared milestones and peer support — achieves <strong>85-96% completion rates</strong> according to research from <a href="https://executiveeducation.wharton.upenn.edu/thought-leadership/wharton-online-insights/cohort-vs-self-paced-learning/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Wharton</a> and the University of California.</p>
<p>For adults 50+, the cohort model is even more critical. <a href="https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sociology/articles/10.3389/fsoc.2024.1432607/full" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Research published in Frontiers in Sociology</a> found that older adults learning from same-age peers outperform those taught by younger instructors. The peer model reduces tech anxiety and builds confidence faster than any curriculum alone.</p>
<p>That&#8217;s why 50+TechBridge doesn&#8217;t sell courses. <strong>We deploy cohorts.</strong> A group of 20-50 adults 50+ who learn AI together over 6 weeks with peer support, structured milestones, and real-time progress tracking. And when a cohort finishes, the strongest graduates become peer mentors for the next one — that&#8217;s how we scale without an army of trainers.</p>
