## PROPOSAL 1: Net Inclusion 2027
**Conference:** Net Inclusion 2027 (National Digital Inclusion Alliance)
**Location:** Columbus, OH — February 23–25, 2027
**DEADLINE:** June 29, 2026 (midnight PST)
**Submit:** https://www.digitalinclusion.org/netinclusion2027/
**Format:** Breakout Session Proposal

---

### Session Title
**Bridging the Grey Digital Divide: How One Solo Founder Deployed AI Training Across 13 Locations for Adults 50+**

### Session Description
117 million Americans are over 50. They control $76 trillion in spending power. And most digital literacy programs still teach them how to use email.

50+TechBridge is the only program in America teaching AI and digital skills specifically to adults 50+ — free, hands-on, and deployed through public libraries, senior centers, and community organizations. Founded by Brian McKinney — a 65-year-old solo CEO who manages 30+ AI-powered workflows — the program has trained 264+ adults across 13 locations in Central Texas with a targeted 3x completion rate.

This session shares the replicable model: how to design age-appropriate AI curriculum, deploy it through existing community institutions, and scale it with a Train-the-Trainer approach. Attendees will leave with a practical framework they can adapt for their own communities — whether urban, rural, or tribal.

We'll cover:
- Why traditional digital literacy programs fail adults 50+ (and what works instead)
- How to partner with libraries and senior centers for free venue access
- Curriculum design principles: no jargon, no prerequisites, hands-on from day one
- The Train-the-Trainer model: how 500 facilitators can train 50,000 people
- Funding strategies: WIOA, AARP, local arts/culture grants, and private foundations
- How AI tools can power a solo-founded nonprofit at scale

### Session Takeaways
1. A proven, replicable framework for deploying digital literacy training for adults 50+ through community institutions
2. Practical curriculum design principles that eliminate the "jargon barrier" keeping older adults from engaging with technology
3. Funding strategies and partnership models that make 50+ digital literacy programs sustainable without large staff

### Speaker Bio
Brian McKinney is the founder of 50+TechBridge (Learn More Technologies) — the only program in America teaching AI skills specifically to adults 50+. At 65, he manages 30+ AI-powered workflows as a solo CEO and has deployed free AI training across 13 locations in Central Texas, including 9 Austin Public Library branches, 3 senior centers, and a veterans program. He publishes Agentic50 Weekly — AI news translated for adults 50+ — and speaks on ageism, AI adoption, and the $76 trillion opportunity the tech industry is ignoring. MBE-certified. WIOA-eligible.

### Target Audience
Digital inclusion practitioners, library program managers, senior center coordinators, workforce development professionals, community-based organizations serving older adults, policymakers working on digital equity.

### Why This Matters for Net Inclusion
Digital inclusion conversations often focus on broadband access, affordability, and youth. Adults 50+ — the fastest-growing demographic — are consistently left out. This session fills that gap with a proven model, real numbers, and a speaker who IS the demographic he serves. The grey digital divide is real, and this is how we close it.

## PROPOSAL 4: TEDx (Universal — Submit to Multiple Events)
**Submit to:** 5–10 TEDx events simultaneously
**Search events:** https://www.ted.com/tedx/events
**Format:** 12–18 minute talk

---

### Talk Title
**I'm 65 and AI Runs My Company. Here's Why That Should Scare Silicon Valley.**

### The Idea Worth Spreading
The tech industry has decided that people over 50 can't learn AI. They're wrong — and that assumption is the most expensive blind spot in business today. 117 million Americans over 50 control $76 trillion in spending power, yet virtually no AI products, training programs, or digital tools are built for them. One 65-year-old solo founder is proving the entire industry wrong — and building a global movement in the process.

### Talk Outline (12 minutes)

**Opening (2 min):**
"Two years ago, I'd never heard of ChatGPT. Today, I manage 30 AI workflows. I have no employees. I'm 65 years old. And the tech industry says people like me don't exist."

**The Problem (3 min):**
The numbers — 117 million Americans over 50. $76 trillion in spending power. The least invested-in demographic in tech. While companies spend billions chasing 20-somethings, the fastest-growing market on the planet is invisible.

The word for this is ageism. And in tech, it's not just accepted — it's the business model.

**The Story (3 min):**
How Brian went from "What's ChatGPT?" to running an entire organization with AI at 65. The friend who wanted to digitize her business but couldn't find anyone who'd teach her without jargon. The moment he realized: if he could learn this, anyone could — but no one was teaching it to people like him.

**The Solution (2 min):**
50+TechBridge. Free AI training. Libraries, senior centers, community organizations. 13 locations. 264+ adults trained. No jargon. No prerequisites. No age limit. And now — a global model: Train 500 facilitators who each train 100 people. That's 50,000 adults. That's a movement.

**The Challenge (2 min):**
"The tech industry wrote off 117 million people. I'm one of them. And I'm standing here because I didn't wait for permission. The question isn't whether people over 50 can learn AI. The question is: why did we ever think they couldn't?"

### Why This Idea Matters
Ageism is the last socially acceptable form of discrimination. The digital divide for older adults is global, growing, and almost entirely ignored. This talk gives the audience a new lens: the biggest opportunity in tech isn't a new algorithm. It's the people we stopped believing in.

### Speaker Bio (Short — for TEDx applications)
Brian McKinney, 65, is the founder of 50+TechBridge — the only program in America teaching AI skills specifically to adults 50+. A solo CEO running 30+ AI workflows, he's deployed free training across 13 locations in Central Texas. He is the demographic he serves — and living proof that age is not a barrier to innovation.
