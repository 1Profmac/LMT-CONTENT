## Title: I Redesigned a Course in One Weekend Using AI. Here's the Playbook.

People were signing up for my course. Nobody was finishing it.

I teach adults over 50 how to use their phones. Real things. Making text bigger. Setting reminders. Ordering groceries.

My lessons looked like college courses. 20-minute videos. Walls of text. Quizzes.

People started. Nobody finished.

So I gave myself one weekend and three AI tools.

Here's exactly what I did.

---

**TOOL 1: Claude (AI Writing Assistant)**

Claude wrote my lesson scripts in a warm, conversational voice. Not my voice — a 55-year-old female guide voice. Like a neighbor showing you something over the fence.

It also built me a persona document. I stopped calling people "students." I started calling them "Digital Pioneers." That one change shifted everything.

Time: 20 minutes per lesson script.

---

**TOOL 2: HeyGen (AI Avatar)**

I needed an instructor who looks like my audience. Not a 25-year-old influencer. Someone warm. Approachable. 55+.

I found an avatar named Barb. Southern accent. Blue sweater. Looks like she figured out her phone last year and wants to help you.

I pasted the script. Three minutes later, I had a video.

Time: 3 minutes per video.

---

**TOOL 3: Canva (Visual Walkthroughs)**

Instead of recording my phone screen (shaky, messy, notifications popping up), I built each step as a visual slide. Phone mockup. Gold arrows showing where to tap. One action per slide.

The last slide has confetti and says "You're a Digital Pioneer!"

Nobody wants a quiz score. Everybody wants confetti.

Time: 60-90 minutes per lesson.

---

---

**WHAT I LEARNED**

1. The content wasn't the problem. The format was.
2. AI didn't replace my team. I never had a team. AI made it possible.
3. Ship one lesson. Watch the data. Build the rest based on what you learn.
4. Celebrate louder than you test. "I Did It" beats "Score: 3/3."
5. Give people an identity. "Digital Pioneer" finishes courses. "Student" doesn't.
