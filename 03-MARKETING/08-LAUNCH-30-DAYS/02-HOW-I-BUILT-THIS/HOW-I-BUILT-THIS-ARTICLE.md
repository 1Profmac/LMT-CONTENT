# I Redesigned a Course in One Weekend Using AI. Here's Exactly How.

*By Brian McKinney, Founder — 50+TechBridge / Learn More Technologies*

---

## The Problem Nobody Talks About

People were signing up for my course. Nobody was finishing it.

I teach adults over 50 how to use their phones. Not coding. Not apps for twentysomethings. Real things — making text bigger, setting reminders, ordering groceries. Skills that keep people independent.

But my lessons looked like a college course. Long videos of me talking. Walls of text. A quiz at the end that felt like a test. People would start Lesson 1 and disappear.

I knew the content was good. The packaging was the problem.

---

## The Decision

I gave myself one weekend to rebuild everything. Not plan. Not research. Build.

I had three questions:

1. Can I create a warm, friendly instructor without recording myself?
2. Can I build visual phone walkthroughs without touching a phone?
3. Can I do all of this using AI tools I already pay for?

The answer to all three was yes.

---

## The Tools

Here's everything I used and what each tool did:

### Claude (AI Assistant)
**What it did:** Everything I couldn't do fast enough alone.

- Wrote all three lesson scripts in a warm, conversational female voice
- Built a complete "Digital Pioneer" persona document — who my learner is, what they fear, what makes them stay, what makes them quit
- Created HTML mockup slides that look like phone screens (Settings menus, sliders, toggle switches, app stores)
- Wrote the social media launch strategy, email sequences, and this article
- Kept me on task when I started going in circles

**Cost:** Monthly subscription
**Time saved:** Days of writing condensed into hours

### HeyGen (AI Avatar Video)
**What it did:** Gave my course a face that isn't mine.

I needed a warm, approachable instructor. Not a 25-year-old tech influencer. Not a corporate trainer. Someone who looks like the people taking the course.

I found an avatar named Barb. She's in her late 50s. Warm face. Southern accent. Casual blue sweater. She looks like the neighbor who figured out her phone last year and is showing you how.

I pasted my script into HeyGen. Three minutes later, I had a 30-second intro video that sounds like a real person talking to you at your kitchen table.

**Cost:** Monthly subscription
**Time per video:** 3 minutes to generate

### Canva (Visual Walkthroughs)
**What it did:** Replaced screen recordings with animated slide decks.

Instead of fumbling with a phone screen recorder, I built each step as a visual slide:
- A phone mockup frame
- Simplified screens built from shapes (menus, sliders, toggles)
- Gold highlights showing exactly where to tap
- One action per slide
- A celebration screen with confetti at the end

I exported the whole thing as an MP4 video. It looks like a screen recording but cleaner — no notifications popping up, no shaky hands, no mistakes.

**Cost:** Monthly subscription (already had it)
**Time per lesson:** About 60-90 minutes for all slides

## The Persona That Changed Everything

Before I touched a single slide, Claude helped me build a persona document for my learner. I called them the "Digital Pioneer."

A Digital Pioneer is an adult 50+ who:
- Owns a smartphone but uses about 10% of it
- Isn't stupid or scared — just never had a good guide
- Will quit in 30 seconds if something feels like school
- Will stay if they get a win in the first 5 minutes
- Needs to hear "we" not "you should"

That document changed how I wrote every script, designed every slide, and chose every word. "Module" became "lesson." "Submit" became "I Did It." "Student" became "Digital Pioneer."

The identity matters. When someone calls you a Digital Pioneer, you want to finish. When someone calls you Student #4,827, you don't care.

I'm one person. No team. No video editor. No instructional designer. No budget for contractors.

AI didn't replace any of those people. I never had them. AI made it possible to build what I couldn't build alone.

## What I'd Do Differently

1. **Start with the persona, not the content.** I spent months building lessons before I understood who was taking them. The persona doc should be step one.

2. **Ship one lesson first.** I almost built all three before testing anything. Shipping Lesson 1 alone and watching the data saved me from building two more lessons nobody might finish.

3. **Don't record yourself if you don't have to.** I spent days setting up lighting, recording takes, editing videos. An AI avatar delivers the script better than I do on camera — and it takes 3 minutes.

4. **Make the celebration bigger than the quiz.** Nobody wants to take a test. Everybody wants to feel like they accomplished something. Confetti and "You're a Digital Pioneer!" outperforms a score of 3/3 every time.

I built this for adults 50+. But the process works for any audience.
