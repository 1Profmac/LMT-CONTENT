# International Visa Guide — Teaching, Training & Speaking Abroad

## The Key Insight: You're Not a "Teacher" — You're a Consultant

"Teaching" requires work permits in most countries. But what YOU do is:
- **Consulting** — designing programs for organizations
- **Speaking** — keynotes at conferences and events
- **Train-the-Trainer** — short-term workshops for partner orgs

These are different legal categories and much easier to do internationally.

---

## The Legal Structure That Works Everywhere

```
Learn More Technologies (US 501(c)(3))
         |
         |— Contracts with partner org in [country]
         |— Partner org hosts the training
         |— Brian delivers as US-based consultant
         |— Payment flows: Funder → LMT (US) → Brian
         |— Brian is NEVER employed by a foreign entity
         |
         Result: No local work permit needed in most cases
```

You're not "working in Spain" — you're a US-based consultant delivering services under contract. Your income is US-sourced. Your employer is your own US nonprofit.

## Country-by-Country Visa Breakdown

### HOME BASES (Long-Term Living)

#### Spain — Digital Nomad Visa
| Detail | Info |
|---|---|
| **Visa type** | Digital Nomad Visa (Ley de Startups) |
| **Can you consult?** | Yes — freelancers/consultants for foreign clients explicitly covered |
| **Can you teach workshops?** | Gray area — work must be "remote" for foreign clients |
| **The workaround** | Your US nonprofit (LMT) contracts with Spanish partner orgs. You're paid by YOUR company, not theirs |
| **Local client limit** | Up to 20% of revenue from Spanish clients |
| **Income req** | ~€2,850/mo (~$3,100) |
| **Duration** | 1 year (from abroad) or 3 years (from within), renewable to 5 |
| **Tax** | Flat 24% for first 4 years (Beckham Law) |
| **Processing** | 4–6 months from US consulate |
| **Apply at** | Spanish consulate (Houston is closest to Austin) |
| **Action** | Budget €500 for immigration lawyer consult |

#### Portugal — D8 Digital Nomad Visa
| Detail | Info |
|---|---|
| **Visa type** | D8 (Passive Income / Digital Nomad) |
| **Can you consult?** | Yes — freelancers and remote workers covered |
| **Can you teach workshops?** | Same gray area — structure through US entity |
| **Income req** | ~€3,680/mo (~$4,000) |
| **Duration** | 1 year, renewable to 2, then permanent residency pathway |
| **Tax** | NHR regime — potential 20% flat rate |
| **Processing** | 30–60 days |
| **Bonus** | Direct flights to Africa (Angola, Mozambique, Cape Verde) |

#### Panama — Pensionado Visa (Easiest of All)
| Detail | Info |
|---|---|
| **Visa type** | Pensionado (Retiree/Pensioner) |
| **Can you consult?** | Yes — you can own a business and invest |
| **Can you teach?** | Yes — education specifically mentioned as retiree opportunity |
| **Can you work for a local company?** | No — you can OWN a business but can't be an employee |
| **The structure** | LMT contracts with Panamanian partners. You're the US-based consultant |
| **Income req** | $1,000/mo from pension (Social Security counts) |
| **Tax** | ZERO tax on foreign-earned income |
| **Duration** | Permanent residency |
| **Discounts** | 25–50% off flights, hotels, restaurants, medical (age 65+) |
| **Currency** | US Dollar |
| **Brian qualifies automatically** | Age 65 + Social Security = done |

### DEPLOYMENT COUNTRIES (Short-Term Trips)

#### Ghana — B1 Business Visa
| Detail | Info |
|---|---|
| **Can you consult/train?** | Yes — consultations, training, presentations explicitly permitted |
| **Can you teach workshops?** | Yes — short-term training under 90 days covered |
| **Payment** | Should come from your US entity, not a Ghanaian employer |
| **Duration** | 30–60 days, extendable to 90 |
| **Model** | Fly in → deliver Train-the-Trainer (1–2 weeks) → fly out |
| **Requirements** | Valid passport, invitation letter from host org, proof of funds |

#### Kenya — E-Visa (Business)
| Detail | Info |
|---|---|
| **Can you consult/train?** | Yes — business meetings, training, consulting permitted |
| **Duration** | 90 days (e-visa) |
| **Model** | Same as Ghana — short deployment, local team sustains |
| **Apply** | Online at evisa.go.ke |

#### South Africa — No Visa Needed
| Detail | Info |
|---|---|
| **Entry** | 90 days visa-free for US citizens |
| **Can you consult/train?** | Short-term business activities permitted |
| **Model** | Conference speaking + Train-the-Trainer |

## Visa Priority Order

1. **Get the 501(c)(3) first** — this is your legal entity for all international contracts
2. **First trips: tourist/business visas** — attend conferences, make connections (no visa app needed for most)
3. **First home base: Spain or Panama** — apply after 501(c)(3) is approved
4. **Africa deployments: business visas** — short-term, as needed

---

## What to Prepare Now (Before Any Visa Application)

- [ ] Passport — valid for 6+ months beyond travel date, 2+ blank pages
- [ ] 501(c)(3) determination letter (proves you're a nonprofit, not a worker)
- [ ] Proof of income (bank statements, Social Security letter, grant awards)
- [ ] Private health insurance (required for Spain/Portugal)
- [ ] Professional portfolio / website (shows you're a consultant, not seeking employment)
- [ ] Letters from partner organizations (invitation letters for business visas)
