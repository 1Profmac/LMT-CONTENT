# 45-Minute Library Lab — Don't Get Scammed
**50+TechBridge · Facilitator Guide**

---

## Overview

**Format:** In-person, small group (8–20 participants)
**Time:** 45 minutes
**Location:** Library computer lab or meeting room
**Materials:** Printed fridge cards, printed family one-pagers, whiteboard or flip chart

---

## Facilitator Setup (10 min before)

- Print one fridge card per participant (03-fridge-card.md)
- Print one family one-pager per participant (04-family-one-pager.md)
- Write on whiteboard: *Unexpected + hurry + money or a code = hang up and verify*
- Cue up one obviously fake email example on the projector (labeled FAKE)

---

## Session Flow

### Welcome (3 min)

Introduce yourself. Tell participants:
- "This is not a lecture about how dangerous the internet is."
- "This is three skills. You will leave with a card for your fridge."
- "You are allowed to ask anything. There are no bad questions here."

---

### Skill 1 — Spot a Fake (10 min)

**Show** the fake email on screen. Ask the group:
- "What feels off about this?"
- "Who started this conversation?"
- "Where does that link actually go?"

**Teach:**
- Look at the sender address, not the logo
- "Dear Customer" from a bank that knows your name is a tell
- AI made the spelling better — typos are no longer a safety test

**Social media add-on (2 min):**
- FTC 2025: $2.1 billion started on social media. Facebook leads in volume.
- Fake ads, taken-over friend accounts, fake giveaways
- Same rule applies: who started this?

**Group activity:** Show two side-by-side emails (one real, one fake, both labeled). Ask participants to point out the tells. 3 minutes.

---

### Skill 2 — Name the Pressure (10 min)

Write on the board: **Hurry. Fear. Secrecy. Authority.**

Ask: "Has anyone received a call that felt like one of these?"
- Let 2–3 people share briefly. Do not push.

**Teach the fifth lever:** "Never move money to protect it."
- No bank, agency, or grandchild story needs a gift card or a Bitcoin ATM.

**Payment stop list:** Write on board — gift cards, wire, crypto, courier, remote access.

Ask: "Which of these have you seen or heard of?" Brief show of hands.

---

### Skill 3 — Lock the Door (8 min)

**Passwords:**
- Length beats complexity. A passphrase is stronger than P@ssw0rd!
- Email gets a unique password. Email is the skeleton key.
- Notebook in a drawer beats one reused password on the internet.

**2FA:**
- Settings → Security → Two-factor / 2-step verification
- Turn it on for email this week. Then the bank.
- Never read the code to anyone who calls you.

**Quick demo** (if computers available): Walk participants to Settings → Security on Gmail or their email provider. 3 minutes.

---

### Family Code Word (5 min)

Explain voice cloning in plain language:
- "A few seconds of video or voicemail can be enough to clone a voice."
- "This is a 20th-century fix for a 21st-century trick."

Have participants write their code word on the fridge card right now.
Ask: "Who will you share it with this week?" Brief partner share.

---

### If It Already Happened (3 min)

Read the six steps aloud slowly:
1. Stop sending
2. Call the bank — number on the card
3. Say wire / gift card / crypto if that was used
4. Change email password, turn on 2FA
5. Report: ReportFraud.ftc.gov and ic3.gov
6. Tell one trusted person

"This is not a moral failure. Professionals ran a play on a human nervous system."

---

### Close and Hand Out Cards (6 min)

Hand out fridge cards and family one-pagers.

Say:
- "Put this on your actual fridge tonight."
- "Share the one-pager with one person this week — a neighbor, a sibling, a friend."
- "The next session is free. Bring someone who always clicks."

Write on board one final time: **Hang up. Look up. Call back.**

Take questions. Stay 5 minutes after for one-on-one questions.

---

## Facilitator Notes

- Do not use horror-story tone. Frightened people close down.
- If someone shares a scam they fell for — validate, do not shame.
- Sources stay separate: FTC numbers and FBI numbers are different databases. Never combine them.
- Refer to ReportFraud.ftc.gov and ic3.gov — not generic "report it online."
