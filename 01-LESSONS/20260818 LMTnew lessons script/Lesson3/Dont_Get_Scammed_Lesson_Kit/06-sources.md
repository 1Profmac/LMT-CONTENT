# Sources — Don't Get Scammed (Lesson 3)
**DO NOT MIX FTC AND FBI TOTALS — cite each separately**

---

## FTC Sources

**FTC · Protecting Older Consumers 2024–2025**
- $2.4 billion reported lost by adults 60+ in 2024
- Approximately 4× the 2020 figure
- About 3 in 4 older adults who reported a scam lost no money
- Phone-started fraud: median loss ~$2,200 for older adults
- Adults 50+ are 5× more likely than younger adults to report losing money on tech-support scams
- "Never move money to protect it" guidance

**FTC · August 2025 Impersonation Spotlight**
- $100,000+ impersonation losses rose eightfold from 2020 to 2024

**FTC · 2025 Social Media Fraud Report**
- $2.1 billion in fraud losses started on social media
- Facebook leads in fraud volume, beating text and email

**FTC · Official guidance (ongoing)**
- The FTC will never threaten you, tell you to move money to "protect it," or send you to a Bitcoin ATM
- Never share a verification code with anyone who calls or texts you

Cite as: FTC *Protecting Older Consumers 2024–2025* and FTC social media spotlight

---

## FBI Sources

**FBI Internet Crime Complaint Center (IC3) · 2024 Annual Report**
- Adults over 60 lost nearly $5 billion and filed the most complaints of any age group
- Investment fraud, tech support fraud, and romance scams are top categories
- Hundreds of millions in additional tech-support losses tracked separately

Cite as: FBI IC3 2024 Annual Report

*Note: FTC and FBI use different reporting systems and different populations. Do not add these totals together.*

## AARP Sources

- Approximately 84–85% of adults 50+ are worried about cloned voices and deepfake video
- 95% received at least one scam robocall

Cite as: AARP AI Fraud and Robocall Survey

---

## Microsoft + AARP + Better Business Bureau

- Scams flagged as AI-enabled (realistic voices or video) jumped approximately 20× from 2023 to 2025
- Based on analysis of 500,000+ fraud reports

Cite as: Microsoft, AARP, and BBB joint analysis 2025

## NIST and CISA

**NIST Password Guidance (current)**
- Longer passwords are better — aim for 15+ characters
- A passphrase beats P@ssw0rd!
- You do not have to change passwords every 90 days
- Turn on a second factor

**CISA · Multi-Factor Authentication**
- Any second factor is better than none
- Authenticator app or passkey is stronger than text message
- Text messages can be stolen — still better than nothing

Cite as: NIST Digital Identity Guidelines; CISA MFA guidance
