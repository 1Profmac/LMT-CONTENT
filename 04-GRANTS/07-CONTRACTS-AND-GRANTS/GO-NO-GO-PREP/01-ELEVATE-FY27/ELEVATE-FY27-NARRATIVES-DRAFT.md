## Question 1: Mission & Vision
*What is your mission or artistic vision and what do you do to support it?*

We see all people — especially the ones others ignore. That belief is the foundation of everything we do.

LearnMoreTechnologies.com / 50PlusTechBridge exists to close the gap between Austin's older adults and the AI-driven world they live in — not by pushing technology on people, but by building community around it.

Our vision is a city where age is not a barrier to creative and cultural participation. Artificial intelligence is the defining creative medium of this era — it is reshaping how art is made, how stories are told, how people connect, and how communities protect themselves. If older Austinites cannot access or understand these tools, they are effectively excluded from the cultural life of the city they built.

We support this mission through six structured community cohorts per year, each offering three connected layers of experience:

**Layer 1 — In-Person Gathering:** A 2-hour facilitated public experience at an Austin library or senior center. Open to all. No device required. Participants explore AI tools through their own stories, questions, and creative goals — writing family histories, creating digital art, navigating health information, and protecting themselves from AI-driven scams.

**Layer 2 — Weekly Online Mastermind:** Six weeks of Tuesday or Thursday evening gatherings where cohort members return as a community — asking questions, sharing discoveries, supporting one another, and going deeper into the tools they were introduced to in person. This is where the real learning happens: peer-to-peer, in their own voices.

**Layer 3 — Continuing Education + SecureStart.app:** Every participant receives free lifetime access to SecureStart.app — our AI-powered digital safety platform built for adults 50+ — along with self-paced online lessons and content they can return to anytime.

Our artistic values are accessibility, authenticity, and dignity. We meet every person at their own level. We honor the wisdom they already carry. Access to these services — the in-person gatherings, the online mastermind community, the continuing education content, and SecureStart.app — is provided completely free to every participant. Because belonging in modern Austin should not have a price tag.

## Question 2: Proposed Activity
*What is your proposed activity and how does it reflect the people, places, or cultures of Austin?*

We are proposing six community cohorts — "Digital Pioneer" cohorts — delivered across underserved Austin neighborhoods over 12 months. Each cohort is a six-week community journey rooted in Austin's people, places, and cultures.

**The Cohort Structure:**

Each of our six cohorts follows this arc:

- **Week 1 — In-Person Launch (2 hours):** A free public gathering at an Austin Public Library branch or senior center in neighborhoods including East Austin, Rundberg, North Loop, Southeast Austin, and South Congress. Participants are introduced to AI tools through their own life stories and creative goals. No prior experience required. Walk-ins welcome.
- **Weeks 2–6 — Online Mastermind (weekly, Tue or Thu evenings):** The cohort reunites online as a community — exploring tools together, troubleshooting challenges, sharing what they've created, and supporting one another. A technical support specialist is available at every session.
- **Ongoing — Continuing Education + SecureStart.app:** Participants keep learning at their own pace through self-guided online lessons and free lifetime access to SecureStart.app, which helps adults 50+ navigate AI tools safely and confidently.

**Why This Reflects Austin:**

This model is built around the people Austin often leaves behind. East Austin's historically Black and Latino communities. North Austin's aging immigrant families. Southeast Austin's working-class seniors who helped build this city. We go to their libraries, their senior centers, their neighborhoods. We don't ask them to come to us.

The mastermind model specifically honors something Austin values: community wisdom. Participants are not students being filled with information. They are Digital Pioneers — people whose lived experience of Austin is itself the most valuable asset in the room. The mastermind is where their voices lead.

SecureStart.app extends this cultural investment beyond the sessions. When an older Austinite can spot an AI-generated scam, use voice AI to stay independent, or create something digitally for the first time — that is a cultural outcome. It is creative empowerment, rooted in Austin's neighborhoods, built to last.

## Question 5: Marketing Plan
*How will you market your proposed activities to Austin residents, visitors, and tourists, including underserved communities?*

Our marketing strategy is community-first and channel-specific. We go where our audience already is — because our audience is not on Instagram, and they do not respond to digital ads.

**Library and Senior Center Networks (primary)**
We work directly with Austin Public Library branch managers and senior center program coordinators to post flyers, add cohorts to their event calendars and newsletters, and make personal announcements at existing programming. These partners have direct relationships with our exact audience — their endorsement is the most effective outreach we have. We provide ready-to-use flyer templates and talking points to every partner location.

**Agentic50 Weekly Newsletter**
Our founder publishes a weekly AI literacy newsletter for adults 50+ with an active Austin subscriber base. Every cohort is announced four weeks in advance with plain-language descriptions, a direct RSVP link, and a clear walk-in welcome message.

**Spanish-Language and Community Radio**
We partner with KAZI 88.7 FM (Austin's community radio station serving historically underserved communities) and Spanish-language radio and print outlets to announce cohorts serving East and Southeast Austin — neighborhoods where many older residents are first-generation immigrants who don't use digital channels.

**Faith Communities and Neighborhood Organizations**
We have existing relationships with Foundation Communities, Gus Garcia Senior Center, and neighborhood associations across Austin's older adult network. These trusted community voices pre-announce each cohort to their own audiences — reaching people who have no relationship with libraries or technology but trust their community organizations.

**Walk-In Welcome Policy and Zero Barrier Enrollment**
Every cohort in-person launch is explicitly walk-in friendly — no registration, no device required, no prior knowledge assumed. The online mastermind and continuing education components have simple one-click access links. Our marketing always leads with the message: "You don't need anything to come. Just show up."

**For Visitors and Tourists**
Cohort in-person sessions are listed on the Austin Public Library public event calendar and CreateAustin.org, which is distributed to hotels and visitor centers citywide. Any Austin visitor is welcome to attend an in-person session.
