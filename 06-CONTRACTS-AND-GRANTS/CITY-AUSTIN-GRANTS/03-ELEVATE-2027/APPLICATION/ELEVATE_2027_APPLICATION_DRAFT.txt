SECTION 1 — ORGANIZATION OVERVIEW
================================================================================

**Learn More Technologies (LMT)** is a certified Minority Business Enterprise (MBE) and
WIOA-eligible creative technology organization based in Austin, Texas. Through our flagship
program **50+TechBridge**, we have built and deployed a cohort-based AI and digital
arts program designed specifically for adults 50 and older.

We operate at the intersection of **creative technology, digital arts, and community access.**
Our participants are **digital artists** — they use AI as a creative tool to produce original
work displayed in public exhibitions, gallery walks, and community showcase events open to
Austin residents and visitors alike.

**We deliver Digital Pioneer gatherings across 9 in-person locations in Central Texas**, including:
- **6 Austin Public Library branches** — St. John, Rundberg, Southeast, Milwood, Manchaca Road, Pleasant Hill
- **AARP-affiliated senior centers**
- **Foundation Communities** — veterans program

LMT received prior ACME funding through a **Nexus Grant (FY24, $5,000)**, which launched
our library-based AI creative training series. The Elevate Grant expands that proven series
into a full cohort-based digital arts program culminating in a **public AI Art Gallery Walk**
that engages Austin residents, tourists, and the broader cultural community.

SECTION 2 — PROJECT DESCRIPTION / CREATIVE NARRATIVE
================================================================================

**PROJECT TITLE: The Digital Pioneer Gallery — AI Art & Technology Exhibitions by Adults 50+**

**50+TechBridge has built a cohort-based digital arts program** that positions adults 50+ as
working artists in Austin's creative ecosystem. Each participant uses AI tools to create
original digital artwork, then presents and exhibits that work at a **public AI Art Gallery Walk**
— a recurring exhibition series held at Austin's premier cultural institutions and open to
the visiting public.

**The creative end product is an original piece of AI-assisted digital art**, produced by
the participant and displayed publicly. The final showcase is not a classroom exercise —
it is a **gallery exhibition**. A public creative act. A contribution to Austin's cultural
and artistic landscape.

**Our program is structured around 5 cohorts per grant year**, each organized by
artistic discipline:
- **Digital Storytelling** — AI-assisted personal and community narratives, visual stories,
  and digital memoir
- **Digital Visual Art** — AI-generated and AI-assisted imagery, digital design, and
  visual creative projects
- **Community Media** — AI-powered newsletters, social campaigns, and digital content
  created for and about Austin communities
- **Creative Technology & Innovation** — AI workflow demonstrations, technology-assisted
  creative tools, and original digital concepts

Each cohort culminates in a **public Gallery Walk exhibition** hosted at a partner cultural
institution — rotating through **The Carver Museum, the Mexican American Cultural Center,
The Contemporary Austin, and Austin Public Library branches** — giving participants access
to Austin's established cultural venues and connecting their work with residents and tourists.

**This is how we expand Austin's creative community.** Adults 50+ are prolific, experienced,
and underserved as artists. **50+TechBridge puts AI tools in their hands and puts their work
on Austin's gallery walls.**

SECTION 3 — ACTIVITIES & STEPS (ITEMIZED)
================================================================================

**The following steps describe the complete creative arc of each cohort, from intake to
public gallery exhibition. Each of the 5 cohorts follows this structured process:**

---

**STEP 1 — COHORT INTAKE & ARTISTIC ORIENTATION (Week 1)**
- Participants recruited through Austin Public Library branches, senior centers,
  community partner organizations, and cultural institution outreach
- Intake session: each participant identifies their **artistic discipline and creative goal**
  for the cohort — what they will make and exhibit
- Guest artist/instructor introduced; volunteer mentors assigned (1 per 2 participants)
- Venue partner confirmed for the cohort's final Gallery Walk exhibition
- **Team:** Lead instructor (Brian McKinney), 1 guest instructor, 2-3 volunteer mentors

---

**STEP 2 — CREATIVE TECHNOLOGY TRAINING (Weeks 2–3)**
- Participants learn the AI tools they will use to create their artwork
- Hands-on Technology Assisted Training sessions, in-person at library or cultural center
- Training is tailored to the cohort's artistic discipline:
  - Digital storytellers: AI writing and narrative tools
  - Visual artists: AI image generation and design tools
  - Community media artists: AI content and publishing tools
- **Volunteer mentors** provide one-on-one support during every session
- **Team:** Lead instructor, 1-2 guest subject-matter instructors, 4-6 volunteer mentors

---

**STEP 3 — ARTWORK DEVELOPMENT WORKSHOP (Weeks 4–6)**
- Participants create their individual artwork with structured guidance and peer input
- Weekly check-ins with mentor; mid-project cohort critique session
- Participants receive written feedback and revision direction
- Works are refined and prepared for gallery display
- **Team:** Brian McKinney, volunteer mentors, peer cohort group

---

**STEP 4 — MARKETING, PRESENTATION & EXHIBITION PREP (Week 7)**
- Dedicated session on **how to market, present, and sell creative work**
- Each participant prepares an artist statement for their exhibition piece
- Training covers: pricing digital work, licensing AI-assisted art, online portfolio setup,
  and social media promotion of their artwork
- Gallery display format finalized; exhibition materials prepared
- **Team:** Guest instructor (professional artist / creative business background),
  volunteer mentors

---

**STEP 5 — PUBLIC AI ART GALLERY WALK (Week 8)**
- **Public exhibition, open to the community and visiting tourists**
- Hosted at a rotating Austin cultural venue:
  **The Carver Museum | Mexican American Cultural Center | The Contemporary Austin |
  Austin Public Library | The Thinkery | LBJ Presidential Library**
- Each participant's work is displayed and the artist is present to discuss their process
- Exhibition is promoted through VisitAustin, venue partner channels, and LMT media
- Guests include community members, library/center patrons, tourists, and partner organizations
- Participants receive a **Digital Pioneer Artist Certificate**
- Event documented (photos, video) for ACME reporting and VisitAustin submission
- **Team:** Full team — lead instructor, guest instructors, all volunteers, venue partner staff

---

**COHORT & EXHIBITION CALENDAR (5 cohorts across grant year):**

| Cohort | Discipline | Venue Partner | Dates (est.) |
|--------|-----------|---------------|--------------|
| 1 | Digital Storytelling | Austin Public Library — Central | Oct – Nov 2026 |
| 2 | Digital Visual Art | The Carver Museum | Nov – Dec 2026 |
| 3 | Community Media | Mexican American Cultural Center | Jan – Feb 2027 |
| 4 | Creative Technology | The Contemporary Austin | Feb – Mar 2027 |
| 5 | All Disciplines (Grand Gallery Walk) | Austin Public Library — Grand Gallery Walk | Apr – May 2027 |

**The final cohort (Cohort 5) is a Grand Gallery Walk** bringing together artists from all
four prior cohorts for a single unified public exhibition — the largest public-facing event
of the grant year, designed to draw Austin residents and tourists and to demonstrate the
full breadth of AI-assisted creative work produced by 50+ digital artists.

  license, and sell their creative work, adding them to Austin's creative economy

**Austin's creative community grows stronger when its 50+ residents are creating and
