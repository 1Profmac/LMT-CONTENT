## #1 Recommendation: Pittsburgh Home Base, Serve Both PA Metros

### Why Pittsburgh Wins

**1. The philanthropy is built for Brian's exact situation right now**

The Pittsburgh Foundation's 2025–26 guidelines explicitly prioritize organizations with budgets under $2M that are "responding to federal policy and/or funding shifts." That is a description of 50+TechBridge after the DEA termination. Applications open February 3 and July 15.

**2. Pennsylvania's state grants are active and NOT DEA-dependent**

Unlike Massachusetts (Launchpad suspended) and Washington State (heavily federal), PA's aging grants draw from state and ARPA HCBS funds — not the terminated DEA. They are open right now.

**3. Two major metros, one base**

Pittsburgh + Philadelphia = two of the country's top aging markets. Pittsburgh home base, Philadelphia delivery via Amtrak ($25–30, 3 hours). You get dual-market reach at single-market operating cost.

**4. Cost efficiency is decisive for a solo founder**

Pittsburgh is 10–15% below national average. Compare: Boston is +30%, NYC is +50%. Longer runway, lower burn, more program dollars per grant dollar.

**5. Library delivery infrastructure is best-in-class**

Carnegie Library of Pittsburgh: 20 neighborhood branches. Free Library of Philadelphia: 54 branches. PA State Library Bureau funds every county. This is 50+TechBridge's primary delivery channel — it's excellent here.

## Region 2: New York State (if budget allows later)

**Why it's #2:** NY replaced federal DEA funding with $50M state commitment (ConnectALL). NYC mandated digital literacy for older adults by law (§ 21-213). Largest absolute market in the country.

**Why it's not #1:** Cost. NYC operating costs would consume 30–50% more of Brian's budget for the same program output. Best play: establish PA first, then expand to NY as a delivery market with NYC-based partners, not as a home base.

**Key NY contacts when ready:**
- Empire State Development ConnectALL Office — $7.4M + $8.3M in awards rounds
- NYC Department for the Aging (DFTA) — vendor opportunities for mandated digital literacy
- New York Public Library — Senior Planet partnership model

## Region 3: Massachusetts

**Best feature:** COA (Council on Aging) grant system. $1.5M "Enhancing Digital Literacy for Older Adults" grant — up to $100K per COA. 350+ Councils on Aging across the state. This is the most purpose-built state grant pipeline for 50+TechBridge's exact model anywhere in the country.

**The problem:** Cost. Boston is 30%+ above national average. Western MA (Springfield, Worcester) is more affordable.

**Best play:** License curriculum to Massachusetts COAs from a Pennsylvania base. You don't need to live there to sell into the COA grant system.

---

## Region 4: Minnesota (Twin Cities)

**Best feature:** Otto Bremer Trust — $28.4M in grants in the first 4 months of 2025 alone. Very accessible to new nonprofits in MN, ND, MT, WI.

**The gap:** No purpose-built older adult digital literacy state grant equivalent to MA or PA. State broadband funding is infrastructure-focused.

**Best play:** Partnership with a Minnesota org to co-apply for Otto Bremer grants. Not a home base.

---

## Region 5: North Carolina (Research Triangle)

**Best features:** Fast-growing 50+ population (retirement migration), low cost, strong library LSTA grant activity ($2.54M June 2025), Research Triangle corporate market (SAS, Cisco, IBM, Red Hat, Lenovo).

**The gap:** Philanthropic infrastructure is thinner. No dominant local foundation with Heinz/Pittsburgh Foundation depth in the digital equity + aging space.

**Best play:** Strong expansion market once established elsewhere. Not a home base.

## What This Means for Austin (Current Base)

Austin has:
- 9 libraries, 3 senior centers already in the network
- Active proof points and local relationships
- Workforce Solutions Capital Area (WIOA ETPL listing in progress)
- Nexus and Elevate grants in pipeline

**Don't abandon Austin.** Keep the Texas operation running — it's your proof of concept and your grant history. The question is whether Brian **adds** a Pittsburgh presence (programs, partnerships, grant applications) or **relocates** entirely.

**Recommended approach:** Establish PA presence first through partnerships and remote grant applications. If Pittsburgh-based grants and contracts materialize ($100K+), evaluate physical presence or part-time base there.
