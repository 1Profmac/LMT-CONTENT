## 1. Tom Kamber — OATS / Senior Planet (Your Closest Model)

**Started:** 2004 — Free 10-week tech course in a Brooklyn housing project
**Now:** National program, AARP charitable affiliate, 100,000+ older adults served, 50 tech centers in 5 states

### The Story
Tom Kamber was working in lower Manhattan after 9/11. He noticed older adults in public housing getting left behind by technology. He started a free 10-week computer course in a Brooklyn housing project. No funding. No nonprofit status. Just him and a room full of seniors.

That course became OATS (Older Adults Technology Services). In 2013, he opened the first tech-themed community center for older adults in America — Senior Planet — in a Chelsea storefront. By 2021, AARP came knocking. OATS became a **charitable affiliate of AARP**, instantly scaling to national reach.

In 2024, OATS received a **$500,000 grant from JPMorganChase** for tech training for older adult small business owners.

### What Brian Can Learn
- He started with ONE free class in ONE housing project. You started with ONE library.
- He didn't scale by teaching more classes himself. He built a **licensing model** — other orgs run Senior Planet programs using his curriculum.
- The AARP affiliation was the rocket fuel. **Your AARP relationship is already in your HubSpot pipeline.**
- He proves that "free tech training for seniors" is a **fundable, scalable, nationally recognized model.**

### The Difference — Your Edge
Tom Kamber built for computers and internet basics in 2004. You're building for **AI** in 2026. His model is 20 years old. Yours is the next generation. You're not competing — you're the upgrade.

**Source:** [OATS About Us](https://oats.org/about-us/) | [Senior Planet Story](https://oats.org/senior-planet-story/)

## 2. Neil Dsouza — GetSetUp (Your Scale Model)

**Started:** 2020 — Two guys on a trail run had an idea
**Now:** 4 million users, 160+ countries, $20M raised, 5.27 million learning sessions in one year

### The Story
Neil Dsouza was a Cisco engineer turned education entrepreneur. He'd already built Zaya Learning Labs — a nonprofit providing education to underprivileged kids in India. After stepping down, he went on a trail run with Lawrence Kosick in the Golden Gate hills.

They realized the 50+ market was completely ignored. Not by accident — by design. Tech companies don't build for older adults because they don't think they'll use it. Neil and Lawrence proved everyone wrong.

GetSetUp launched in February 2020 — right before COVID. Timing was perfect. Older adults were locked at home, desperate to connect and learn. They built a **peer-to-peer model**: older adults teaching older adults. The learner becomes the teacher.

They raised $10M from Reid Hoffman (LinkedIn founder), Jerry Yang (Yahoo founder), and Mark Pincus (Zynga founder). Then another $10M. Now 4 million users in 160 countries.

### What Brian Can Learn
- Neil didn't start in 160 countries. He started with **one audience** (older adults) and **one insight** (they want to learn from peers, not kids).
- The peer-to-peer model works. Your **Train-the-Trainer** model is the in-person version of this.
- GetSetUp is purely virtual. You have the **in-person community deployment** they can't replicate. That's not a weakness — it's your differentiator.
- He was an engineer first, educator second. You're an **educator first** — that's what funders want.

### The Difference — Your Edge
GetSetUp went wide and virtual. You go **deep and local**. Institutions trust someone who shows up in their library. GetSetUp can't do that. You can — in every country.

**Source:** [GetSetUp Founder Story](https://www.getsetup.com/post/the-story-of-getsetup-a-race-to-empower-and-connect-older-adults) | [Neil Dsouza Interview](https://www.adammendler.com/blog/neil-dsouza/)

## 3. Sal Khan — Khan Academy (Your Inspiration Model)

**Started:** 2004 — Tutoring his cousin Nadia over the phone
**Now:** 168 million learners, 190 countries, 50+ languages, backed by Bill Gates

### The Story
In 2004, Sal Khan's 12-year-old cousin Nadia was failing math. He started tutoring her over the phone. Then other relatives wanted help. He recorded a few videos and posted them on YouTube in 2006. He had no plan to build a company.

By 2009, the YouTube channel was growing. He quit his job as a hedge fund analyst to focus on it full-time. In 2010, Bill Gates mentioned Khan Academy publicly — called it "the future of education." Gates Foundation and Google gave millions.

Today: 168 million students. 190 countries. 50+ languages. All from one guy tutoring his cousin.

### What Brian Can Learn
- Sal started with **one person** he cared about. You started because YOU were the person who needed this.
- He didn't ask permission. He didn't apply for grants first. He just **started teaching**.
- The key moment wasn't the YouTube video. It was **Bill Gates saying his name**. Your "Bill Gates moment" could be an AARP endorsement, a UNESCO prize, or a SXSW keynote.
- Khan Academy is a 501(c)(3). Free forever. Funded by philanthropy. **That's your model.**

### The Difference — Your Edge
Khan serves kids and students. Nobody is the "Khan Academy for adults 50+." That's the gap. That's you.

**Source:** [Khan Academy Origin Story](https://newsletter.storytellingforentrepreneurs.com/p/from-tutoring-his-niece-in-his-closet-to-now-serving-168-million-students-the-story-of-khan-academy)

## 4. Sugata Mitra — Hole in the Wall / School in the Cloud (Your TED Model)

**Started:** 1999 — Put a computer in a hole in a wall in a Delhi slum
**Now:** Won $1M TED Prize, schools in India and England, global education movement

### The Story
In 1999, Sugata Mitra was a computer science professor in New Delhi. He cut a hole in the wall between his office and the adjacent slum, stuck a computer in it, and walked away. Hidden cameras showed slum kids — who had never seen a computer — teaching themselves to use it within hours. Then teaching each other.

This became the "Hole in the Wall" experiment. Mitra proved that children can teach themselves almost anything if given access and encouragement — without formal instruction.

He gave a TED talk. Then another. Then in 2013, he won the **$1 million TED Prize** — the largest education prize in the world. He used it to build "School in the Cloud" — self-organized learning environments in India and England, supported by volunteer "Grannies" on webcam.

### What Brian Can Learn
- His entire global movement started with **one experiment on one wall**. Your movement started with one workshop in one library.
- He didn't scale by building more walls. He built a **model** (Self-Organized Learning Environments — SOLEs) that anyone can replicate.
- The TED talk changed everything. **One talk, filmed well, can launch a global career.**
- His "Grannies" — retired volunteers who mentor kids via webcam — is literally your Train-the-Trainer model. Older adults supporting learners.

### The Difference — Your Edge
Mitra proved kids can self-teach. You're proving **adults 50+ can learn AI** — which the world thinks is impossible. Your story is just as powerful. Maybe more, because you ARE the demographic you serve.

**Source:** [TED Prize — Sugata Mitra](https://blog.ted.com/a-school-in-the-cloud-sugata-mitra-accepts-the-ted-prize-at-ted2013/) | [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sugata_Mitra)

## 5. Fred Swaniker — African Leadership Group (Your Africa Model)

**Started:** 2004 — One leadership academy in South Africa
**Now:** 4 organizations, 46 sites, 20 countries, TIME 100 Most Influential, training 3 million leaders

### The Story
Fred Swaniker grew up fleeing political violence in Ghana. As a child, he hid under his bed while soldiers roamed his neighborhood. He became convinced that Africa's problems were leadership problems.

At 17, he ran a high school his mother founded. He went to Stanford for his MBA, wrote a business plan for a pan-African leadership academy, and launched the African Leadership Academy in 2004 in South Africa with his classmate as co-founder.

From one school, he built the African Leadership Group — now spanning an academy, a university, a leadership network, and a consulting firm. Terra Education, his latest venture, provides leadership training across 46 sites in 20 countries in Latin America, Asia, Africa, and Europe. TIME named him one of the 100 Most Influential People in the World.

### What Brian Can Learn
- He started with **one school in one country**. Now he's in 20 countries across 4 continents.
- He didn't try to do everything alone. He built **an ecosystem** — multiple organizations, each serving a different function.
- The key was the **Train-the-Trainer** mindset: "I can't train 3 million leaders myself. But I can train the people who will."
- He proved a model in Africa, then **exported it globally** to Latin America, Asia, and Europe.

### The Difference — Your Edge
Fred is building for young leaders in their 20s. You're building for experienced adults in their 50s–70s. His audience has time but no experience. Yours has experience but no access. Both are underserved. Both are global.

**Source:** [Fred Swaniker — CEO Magazine](https://www.theceomagazine.com/business/management-leadership/fred-swaniker-african-leadership-group/) | [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fred_Swaniker)

## 6. Leila Janah — Samasource (Your "Give Work, Not Aid" Model)

**Started:** 2008 — Wondered why digital jobs couldn't go to people in poverty
**Now:** 50,000+ people lifted out of poverty, working with 25% of Fortune 100, raised $14.8M

### The Story
Leila Janah was working as a consultant for an outsourcing firm that hired middle-class Indian workers for digital tasks like data annotation. She asked a simple question: "Why can't the poor do this work too?"

In 2008, she founded Samasource — training people in Kenya, Uganda, and India in digital skills, then connecting them to real paying work from companies like Microsoft, Google, and Walmart. Not charity. Work.

She also founded Samaschool — a nonprofit training people in digital skills for the gig economy. And LXMI — a fair-trade skincare company sourcing from women in East Africa.

Leila passed away in 2020 at age 37. Her organizations continue to operate. 50,000+ people lifted out of poverty through digital work.

### What Brian Can Learn
- She asked one question — "why not them?" — and built a global organization around the answer. Your question is "why not us?" — why not adults 50+?
- She proved **digital skills training → economic opportunity**. That's your model.
- Samaschool is exactly your concept — a nonprofit training underserved people in digital skills. She proved it works in Africa. You can prove it works for 50+ globally.
- She built multiple ventures around one mission. **LMT + 50+TechBridge + Agentic50** — you're already doing this.

## 8. Colonel Harland Sanders — KFC (Your "It's Never Too Late" Model)

**Started:** Age 62 — broke, with a recipe and a car
**Now:** The most recognized fast food brand on Earth

### The Story
At 62, Harland Sanders had just lost his restaurant when a new interstate bypassed his town. He was broke. He had a Social Security check and a chicken recipe.

He got in his car and drove to restaurants across America, cooking his chicken for owners and asking them to franchise his recipe. He was rejected **1,009 times** before getting his first yes.

By 1964 — two years later — KFC had 600 locations. He sold the company for what would be $9.2 billion today.

### What Brian Can Learn
- He was 62 and broke. You're 65 and building. You're already ahead of the Colonel.
- He didn't open 600 restaurants. He **licensed his recipe**. You're not going to teach 50,000 people. You're going to **license your curriculum**.
- He was rejected 1,009 times. You've been rejected zero times — because you haven't started asking big yet. Start asking.
- The car trip = your speaking circuit. He drove to restaurants. You'll fly to countries.
